What Happens to the Body in a Burial Vault

Burial vaults are a common feature in modern cemeteries, but many people wonder what actually happens to the body in a burial vault over time. This guide will walk you through the purpose of burial vaults, the process of decomposition inside a vault, and the key factors that influence what occurs after interment. Understanding Burial Vaults

Burial vaults are sturdy containers, typically made of concrete, metal, or plastic, that surround the casket in the ground. Their main purpose is to prevent the grave from collapsing and to protect the casket from soil and water. While some believe that vaults preserve the body, their primary function is structural rather than preservative.

What Happens to the Body Over Time?

After burial, the body in a burial vault undergoes natural decomposition. The process is influenced by several factors, including the type of casket, the tightness of the vault seal, soil conditions, and environmental moisture. Contrary to popular belief, burial vaults do not stop decomposition; they may slow it slightly by limiting exposure to soil and water, but the process continues within the sealed environment.

Decomposition typically follows these stages:

  • Initial Decay: Begins within days, as bacteria break down soft tissues.
  • Putrefaction: Gases build up, and the body may bloat as tissues liquefy.
  • Dry Decay: Over months to years, only bones and tougher tissues remain.

Common Misconceptions

Many people assume that a burial vault will keep the body perfectly preserved for decades or even centuries. In reality, while vaults do provide some protection, they cannot prevent the natural breakdown of organic matter. Over time, even in the most secure vaults, the body will decompose. Vaults are required by many cemeteries mainly to maintain the appearance and safety of the burial ground, not to preserve remains indefinitely.
