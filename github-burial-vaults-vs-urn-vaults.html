Burial Vaults vs Urn Vaults: Understanding the Differences

When planning a burial or cremation, families often encounter the terms burial vault and urn vault. While both serve to protect remains and preserve the integrity of the gravesite, they are designed for different purposes and situations. This guide will help you understand the key differences between burial vaults vs urn vaults, so you can make an informed choice for your loved one or pre-planning needs.

What Is a Burial Vault?

A burial vault is a lined and sealed outer container that houses a casket in a grave. Its primary purpose is to prevent the ground above from collapsing and to protect the casket from soil, moisture, and outside elements. Burial vaults are typically required by many cemeteries to maintain the landscape and ensure safety. They are most commonly constructed from concrete, metal, or high-strength plastic, and may include decorative finishes or personalized touches.

What Is an Urn Vault?

An urn vault is a protective container designed specifically for cremation urns. Like burial vaults, urn vaults shield the urn from soil and water, and help prevent the ground from sinking over time. These vaults are generally smaller than burial vaults and are made from similar materials, such as concrete, polymer, or metal. Urn vaults are used when cremated remains are to be interred in a cemetery plot, columbarium, or memorial garden.

Choosing the Right Vault for Your Needs

When deciding between a burial vault and an urn vault, consider the type of final disposition you are planning. If you are arranging a traditional burial with a casket, a burial vault is likely required by the cemetery. For cremation remains, an urn vault is the appropriate choice if you plan to bury the urn in a plot or memorial garden. Always check with your chosen cemetery for their specific requirements, as rules can vary widely.

Frequently Asked Questions

Are burial vaults and urn vaults required by law?

Neither burial vaults nor urn vaults are required by federal or state law in the United States, but many cemeteries have their own regulations that mandate their use. Always confirm requirements with your cemetery before making a purchase.

Can urn vaults be personalized?

Yes, many urn vaults can be customized with engravings, emblems, or special finishes. This allows families to create a meaningful tribute that reflects their loved one.
