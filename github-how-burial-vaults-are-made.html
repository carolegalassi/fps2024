How Burial Vaults Are Made

Burial vaults play a crucial role in modern funerals, providing both structural support for the grave and protection for the casket. Understanding how burial vaults are made can help families make informed choices and appreciate the craftsmanship involved in this important aspect of burial traditions. This guide explores the step-by-step process, materials used, and quality standards that define today’s burial vaults.

What Is a Burial Vault?

A burial vault is a lined and sealed outer container designed to protect the casket from the weight of the earth and heavy maintenance equipment that may pass over the grave. Vaults also help prevent the ground from settling, keeping cemeteries looking neat and safe for visitors. Vaults are typically required by many cemeteries and are available in a variety of materials and finishes.

Materials Used in Burial Vault Construction

The choice of materials is essential to the durability and effectiveness of a burial vault. The most common materials include:

  • Concrete: The most widely used material, often reinforced with steel for added strength.
  • Metal: Steel, bronze, and copper are sometimes used for premium vaults.
  • Plastic or Polymer Liners: Used as inner linings to add waterproofing and chemical resistance.

Step-by-Step: How Burial Vaults Are Made

  1. Design and Mold Preparation: Vaults begin with a carefully designed mold, typically made from steel or fiberglass. The mold determines the final shape and surface texture of the vault.
  2. Mixing Materials: For concrete vaults, a precise mix of cement, sand, gravel, water, and sometimes steel reinforcement is prepared. The mix must meet strict standards for strength and durability.
  3. Pouring and Curing: The concrete is poured into the mold and vibrated to remove air pockets. It is then left to cure, often for several days, to achieve maximum strength.
  4. Lining and Sealing: Many vaults receive an inner liner made of polymer or metal, which is bonded to the concrete. Seals and gaskets are added to create a watertight barrier.
  5. Finishing Touches: The vault is removed from the mold, inspected for defects, and sometimes painted or covered with decorative finishes. Handles and nameplates may be added as well.

Quality Control and Standards

Manufacturers of burial vaults adhere to strict industry standards. Each vault is inspected for structural integrity, watertightness, and finish quality. Many companies offer warranties and certifications to assure families of the vault’s performance over time. Innovations in materials and manufacturing continue to improve the longevity and reliability of modern burial vaults.
