What Is a Burial Vault?

When planning a burial, families often encounter the term burial vault. But what exactly is a burial vault, and why do cemeteries require them? This guide provides a comprehensive look at burial vaults, their purpose, construction, and how they fit into modern funeral practices. Whether you are pre-planning or making arrangements for a loved one, understanding burial vaults can help you make informed decisions.

Understanding Burial Vaults

A burial vault is a sturdy outer container that surrounds a casket once it is placed in the ground. Its primary function is to protect the casket from the weight of the soil and heavy cemetery maintenance equipment, as well as to prevent the ground above from sinking over time. Burial vaults are commonly required by cemeteries to maintain the appearance and safety of the burial grounds.

Burial vaults differ from grave liners, which are simpler containers that only cover the top and sides of the casket. Vaults are sealed on all sides, providing greater protection and durability. They are typically made from concrete, metal, or high-strength plastic, and may be reinforced with additional materials for extra strength.

Why Are Burial Vaults Used?

  • To prevent the collapse of the grave and maintain level cemetery grounds
  • To protect the casket from water, soil, and outside elements
  • To comply with cemetery regulations and ensure safe groundskeeping

How Burial Vaults Work

Once a casket is lowered into the grave, the burial vault is placed around it and sealed. This seal helps prevent water and soil from entering the vault, offering extra protection for the casket. The vault also distributes the weight of the soil and any equipment passing over the grave, reducing the risk of ground settling or collapse. Over time, this helps cemeteries maintain a neat, level appearance and ensures the safety of visitors and staff.

Are Burial Vaults Required?

Most cemeteries in the United States require some form of outer burial container, either a vault or a liner. Requirements vary by location and cemetery policy. Vaults are not legally required by federal or state law, but cemetery rules often make them mandatory. If you are unsure, check with your chosen cemetery or funeral provider for their specific regulations.

Choosing the Right Burial Vault

When selecting a burial vault, consider the following:

  • Material and strength
  • Sealing and protective features
  • Cost and budget
  • Cemetery requirements
  • Personal or family preferences

Some families choose enhanced vaults with decorative finishes or personalized touches. Others opt for basic models to meet cemetery rules. Funeral homes and vault providers can explain your options and help you compare features and pricing.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • Can I choose not to use a burial vault? In most cases, cemeteries require at least a grave liner. Check with your cemetery for their specific rules.
  • Are burial vaults environmentally friendly? Some vaults are made with eco-friendly materials, but traditional concrete and metal vaults are not biodegradable. Ask about green burial options if this is a concern.
  • How much does a burial vault cost? Prices range from several hundred to several thousand dollars, depending on materials and features. Compare options to find what fits your needs and budget.

Conclusion

Burial vaults play a vital role in modern burials by protecting caskets, preserving cemetery grounds, and meeting regulatory requirements. Understanding your options can help you make the right choice for your family and honor your loved one with dignity.
