What Happens To A Body Inside A Masoleum?

When considering final resting places, many families wonder what actually happens to a body inside a masoleum. Unlike traditional in-ground burials, masoleums offer above-ground entombment, which can affect the decomposition process, preservation, and even the long-term care of the remains. This guide explores the journey of a body inside a masoleum, what families can expect, and how this choice compares to other burial options.

Understanding Masoleums and Entombment

A masoleum is a free-standing structure designed to house the remains of the deceased above ground. Bodies are placed in crypts, which are sealed chambers within the masoleum. This method of interment has been used for centuries, often chosen for its perceived dignity, protection from the elements, and family legacy. Modern masoleums can be private or communal, and may be located in cemeteries or as stand-alone buildings.

What Happens After Entombment?

Once a body is placed inside a masoleum crypt, it is typically encased in a casket and then sealed with a durable front, often made of marble or granite. The crypt is designed to be airtight or semi-airtight, depending on the construction and regulations. Over time, the body undergoes natural processes of decomposition, but the environment inside a masoleum is quite different from that underground.

Decomposition and Preservation Inside a Masoleum

The decomposition process inside a masoleum is influenced by several factors:

  • Sealing: Airtight crypts can slow down decomposition by limiting oxygen and moisture, sometimes leading to mummification or adipocere formation (a waxy substance).
  • Embalming: Most bodies placed in masoleums are embalmed, which further delays decomposition and helps control odors.
  • Temperature: Masoleums are usually cooler and less humid than the ground, contributing to slower breakdown of remains.

In rare cases, if a crypt is not properly ventilated, gases from decomposition can build up, potentially causing the crypt front to become dislodged. Modern masoleums are designed with venting systems to prevent this issue.

Family Considerations and Visitation

One of the main reasons families choose a masoleum is the ability to visit loved ones in a clean, sheltered environment. Masoleums often feature benches, flowers, and memorial plaques, making them peaceful places for remembrance. Additionally, above-ground entombment can be preferable in areas with high water tables or rocky soil.

Comparing Burial Options

Choosing between a masoleum and traditional burial involves considering cost, tradition, climate, and family preferences. Masoleums may offer more protection from the elements and easier visitation, but can be more expensive and require ongoing maintenance. Conclusion

Understanding what happens to a body inside a masoleum can help families make informed decisions about their loved ones' final resting places. While decomposition is slower and more controlled than in-ground burial, it is important to consider the long-term implications, costs, and maintenance involved. Whether you are planning ahead or making arrangements for a loved one, knowing the facts about masoleums can provide peace of mind and clarity during a difficult time.
