What Is a Burial Vault?

A burial vault is a protective outer container that surrounds a casket in the grave. Its main purpose is to prevent the ground from collapsing over time and to shield the casket from soil and moisture. Burial vaults are typically made from concrete, metal, or plastic, and are designed to provide structural support for the grave. Many cemeteries require the use of a vault or a grave liner to maintain the appearance and safety of the burial grounds.

Why Are Burial Vaults Used?

Burial vaults serve several important functions:

  • They help keep the ground level, preventing sinking or collapse that can occur as the casket and soil settle.
  • They protect the casket from the weight of the earth and heavy cemetery equipment.
  • They reduce maintenance for cemetery staff and help preserve the beauty of the cemetery landscape.

While vaults are not legally required everywhere, most modern cemeteries have made them a standard part of burial due to these benefits.

How to Choose the Right Vault

When selecting a burial vault, consider the cemetery requirements, your budget, and the level of protection you want for the casket. Concrete vaults are the most common, but metal and plastic options are available for families seeking specific features or price points. Always ask your funeral provider for a full list of available vaults and their costs.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • Are burial vaults required by law? In most places, burial vaults are not legally required, but cemeteries often mandate them to maintain the grounds.
  • What is the difference between a vault and a grave liner? A vault is sealed and offers more protection, while a liner is a simpler container that supports the grave but may not be fully sealed.
  • Can I personalize a vault? Many manufacturers offer options for decorative finishes, emblems, and personalized nameplates.
