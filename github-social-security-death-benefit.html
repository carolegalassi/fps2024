Understanding the Social Security Death Benefit

The social security death benefit is a one-time payment provided by the Social Security Administration (SSA) to eligible survivors after a worker passes away. This benefit can help ease the financial burden of funeral expenses and provide immediate support to families during a difficult time. Knowing how this benefit works, who qualifies, and how to apply can make a significant difference for those managing end-of-life affairs.

What Is the Social Security Death Benefit?

The social security death benefit, officially called the Lump-Sum Death Payment (LSDP), is a one-time payment of $255. This benefit is paid to the surviving spouse or, if there is no eligible spouse, to a child of the deceased worker. The benefit is intended to help with immediate expenses following a loved one’s passing, such as funeral or burial costs.

Eligibility Criteria

Not everyone is eligible to receive the social security death benefit. The following table summarizes the main eligibility requirements and who can claim the benefit:

Eligible RecipientRequirementsNotes
Surviving SpouseMust be living with the deceased at the time of death or, if living apart, receiving certain Social Security benefits on the deceased’s recordHas first priority
ChildUnmarried and under 18, or disabled before age 22, or a full-time elementary or secondary school student under 19If no eligible spouse
Other RelativesNot eligibleBenefit limited to spouse or child

How to Apply for the Death Benefit

Applying for the social security death benefit is a straightforward process, but it must be done promptly. The application cannot be completed online. Instead, survivors must contact their local Social Security office or call the SSA at 1-800-772-1213. Required documents typically include the deceased’s Social Security number, proof of death, and the applicant’s own identification and relationship to the deceased.

It is important to apply as soon as possible after the death, as the benefit may not be paid if the claim is delayed for too long. For more details and step-by-step instructions, you can read the full Shopify article or view the Google Doc version.

Frequently Asked Questions

  • Is the death benefit automatic? No, you must apply for the benefit. It is not paid out automatically.
  • Can more than one person receive the benefit? No, the payment is made only once, to the first eligible person who applies.
  • Is the benefit taxable? Generally, the lump-sum death benefit is not taxable income.
