Who Typically Gives a Funeral Eulogy?

The eulogy is one of the most significant moments in a funeral or memorial service. It is an opportunity to reflect on the life, values, and memories of the person who has passed. But who is usually chosen to give this important speech? Traditionally, the eulogy is delivered by someone who had a close relationship with the deceased, but there are no strict rules. The decision is often guided by family wishes, cultural customs, and the comfort level of potential speakers.

Common choices for eulogy speakers include immediate family members such as children, spouses, or siblings. Close friends, colleagues, or spiritual leaders may also be asked. Sometimes, more than one person shares the responsibility, each offering a unique perspective on the life being celebrated.

Factors to Consider When Choosing a Eulogist

  • Relationship to the Deceased: The speaker should have a meaningful connection and be able to share personal stories.
  • Comfort with Public Speaking: Giving a eulogy can be emotionally challenging. Choose someone who feels able to speak in front of others during a difficult time.
  • Wishes of the Deceased: If the person left instructions or expressed preferences, these should be honored.
  • Cultural or Religious Traditions: Some faiths have guidelines about who may speak at a funeral service.

How to Decide Who Should Speak

Choosing the right person to give a eulogy is a personal decision. Some families discuss the options together, while others follow the wishes of the deceased if known. It is not uncommon for more than one person to share the eulogy, dividing the speech into sections or each offering their own tribute. If no one feels able to speak, a celebrant or clergy member can read prepared remarks on behalf of the family.

If you are asked to give a eulogy, remember that sincerity is more important than eloquence. Speak from the heart, share memories, and focus on what made your loved one special. For tips on preparing a eulogy, check out our YouTube channel and The Funeral Program Site.

Conclusion

There is no single answer to the question of who gives a funeral eulogy. The most important thing is that the speaker can honor the life of the deceased with authenticity and respect. Whether you choose a family member, a close friend, or a professional, the eulogy is a chance to celebrate a unique life and offer comfort to those who mourn. Use the resources above to help guide your decision and craft a tribute that truly reflects your loved one.
