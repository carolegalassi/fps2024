Understanding Funeral Procession Etiquette

Funeral processions are a time-honored tradition that allows family and friends to accompany their loved one to their final resting place. The procession typically begins at a funeral home or place of worship and proceeds to the cemetery. Vehicles in the procession are usually marked with flags, headlights, or hazard lights to signal their participation. Observing proper etiquette is not only a sign of respect for the deceased and their family, but it also ensures safety for all involved.

Key Rules for Drivers in a Funeral Procession

  • Always keep your headlights and hazard lights on while in the procession.
  • Stay close to the vehicle in front of you to avoid other cars cutting in.
  • Follow the lead vehicle, usually a hearse, and obey instructions from funeral staff or law enforcement.
  • Do not break away from the procession unless absolutely necessary.
  • Yield to emergency vehicles, even if you are in the procession.

Etiquette for Other Drivers on the Road

  • Yield the right of way to the entire procession, even if traffic signals change.
  • Do not cut into or attempt to pass through a procession.
  • If safe, pull over to the side of the road and allow the procession to pass.
  • Remain patient and respectful, as the delay is usually brief.
  • Never honk or show impatience toward a funeral procession.

Etiquette can vary depending on local laws and customs. In some areas, police escorts are common, while in others, processions rely on the cooperation of other drivers.
