Concrete or Plastic Burial Vaults: What Families Need to Know

When planning a burial, one of the key decisions families face is whether to choose a concrete or plastic burial vault. These vaults are designed to protect the casket from the weight of the earth and from water, helping to maintain the integrity of the gravesite over time. Understanding the differences between concrete and plastic burial vaults can help you make an informed decision that fits your needs, budget, and values.

Understanding Burial Vaults

Burial vaults serve as outer containers for caskets, providing structural support and helping to prevent the grave from sinking or collapsing over time. Most cemeteries require some type of vault or grave liner to maintain the appearance and safety of the grounds. The two most common materials for burial vaults are concrete and plastic (also known as polymer or composite vaults).

Concrete Burial Vaults

Concrete burial vaults have been the industry standard for decades. They are constructed from reinforced concrete and often lined with plastic or metal for added protection. Concrete vaults are known for their strength and durability, making them a popular choice for families who want long-lasting protection for their loved one’s casket. However, concrete vaults are heavy, require special equipment for installation, and can be more expensive than plastic alternatives.

Plastic Burial Vaults

Plastic burial vaults, sometimes called polymer or composite vaults, are made from high-strength plastics or resins. These vaults are much lighter than concrete, making them easier to transport and install. Modern plastic vaults are engineered to be strong and watertight, offering reliable protection at a lower cost. They are also resistant to corrosion and can be a good option for families seeking a more affordable or eco-friendly solution.

Key Considerations for Families

When choosing between concrete or plastic burial vaults, consider the following:

  • Cemetery Requirements: Always check with your cemetery to confirm what types of vaults are permitted.
  • Budget: Concrete vaults tend to be more expensive due to materials and installation, while plastic vaults are often more affordable.
  • Longevity: Both types offer strong protection, but concrete is often chosen for its proven long-term durability.
  • Environmental Concerns: Plastic vaults may have a lower carbon footprint, especially if made from recycled materials.
  • Personal Preference: Some families prefer the traditional feel of concrete, while others appreciate the innovation and practicality of plastic vaults.

Conclusion

Choosing between concrete or plastic burial vaults is a personal decision influenced by budget, cemetery regulations, environmental values, and family preferences. Both types of vaults offer reliable protection for your loved one’s casket, but each comes with its own set of benefits and considerations. Take the time to research your options, consult with your funeral provider, and select the vault that best honors your loved one’s memory and meets your family’s needs.
