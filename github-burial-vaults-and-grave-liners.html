Burial Vaults and Grave Liners: What Families Need to Know

When planning a burial, families often encounter unfamiliar terms and requirements. Two of the most common are burial vaults and grave liners. Understanding what these products are, why cemeteries require them, and how they differ can help you make informed decisions during a difficult time. This guide provides a clear overview of burial vaults and grave liners, their purposes, and what to consider when choosing between them.

What Are Burial Vaults and Grave Liners?

Burial vaults and grave liners are outer containers placed in the ground to surround a casket. Their main purpose is to prevent the grave from collapsing over time and to support the weight of the earth and cemetery maintenance equipment. Many cemeteries require one of these containers for every burial, but the specific type may vary by location.

Burial vaults are sealed containers, usually made of concrete, metal, or plastic, that fully enclose the casket on all sides. They offer the highest level of protection against soil and water intrusion. Grave liners, on the other hand, typically cover only the top and sides of the casket, leaving the bottom open to the earth. They provide basic structural support but less protection from the elements.

Why Do Cemeteries Require These Containers?

The main reason cemeteries require burial vaults or grave liners is to maintain the integrity of the burial ground. Without an outer container, the ground above a casket can settle unevenly as the casket deteriorates. This can create sunken graves, safety hazards, and extra maintenance work. Vaults and liners help keep the cemetery landscape level and safe for visitors and staff.

Some cemeteries specify which type is required, while others allow families to choose. It is important to check with your chosen cemetery before making arrangements.

How to Choose: Factors to Consider

  • Cost: Grave liners are generally less expensive than burial vaults. Vaults with decorative finishes or additional sealing features can be significantly more costly.
  • Protection: If you want maximum protection for the casket and grave site, a burial vault is the better choice. For basic structural support, a grave liner may suffice.
  • Cemetery Rules: Always confirm what your cemetery requires. Some only allow liners, while others may require vaults for certain sections.
  • Personal Preference: Some families prefer the peace of mind that comes with a sealed vault, while others are comfortable with a simpler liner.
